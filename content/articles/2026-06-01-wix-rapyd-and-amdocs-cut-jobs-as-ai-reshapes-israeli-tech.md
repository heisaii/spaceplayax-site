# Wix, Rapyd and Amdocs Cut Jobs as AI Reshapes Israeli Tech

Wix, Rapyd and Amdocs have become the latest Israeli technology companies to announce layoffs as the sector faces growing pressure to reduce costs, tighten operations and reorganize workforces around an increasingly automated business environment.

The announcements add to a broader wave of restructuring across the industry, where companies are trying to adapt to new AI-native ways of working while also managing financial strain. In the case of Wix, the website-building company said it would cut 20% of its global staff, a move that underscores the scale of the adjustment now underway at one of Israel’s best-known tech firms.

The decisions come at a moment when many technology companies are being forced to balance investment in artificial intelligence with a more immediate need for efficiency. Rather than expanding headcount in anticipation of future growth, firms are increasingly rethinking how teams are organized, what tasks can be automated, and which functions can be consolidated. The result has been a sharp focus on operational discipline and a willingness to make deep staffing changes.

Wix’s move appears especially significant because the company is associated with a product category that itself has been transformed by software automation. As a maker of do-it-yourself website building software, the company has long been part of the digital tools ecosystem. Its layoffs now suggest that even businesses closely tied to online services and software infrastructure are not immune to the pressures created by the current AI cycle.

The report also said that the strong shekel has contributed to the cost pressures facing the sector. Currency strength can affect exporters and globally oriented technology companies by raising local operating costs relative to overseas revenue, creating another layer of strain at a time when firms are already contending with changes in product development and internal workflows. For companies with international workforces and global customer bases, this can make the task of managing expenses even more difficult.

Rapyd and Amdocs, according to the report, are following the same general path of tightening operations and reorganizing staff. While the details of each company’s restructuring were not described in the available summary, their inclusion alongside Wix indicates that the pressure is not confined to a single business model or segment. It extends across fintech, software and other parts of the tech industry that depend on large teams, complex systems and steady investment.

The layoffs reflect a broader strategic shift in the sector. AI is no longer being treated only as a product feature or a research priority. It is increasingly shaping the way companies think about labor, productivity and internal structure. Firms are under pressure to prove that they can use automation to do more with fewer resources, and that expectation is changing how executives evaluate staffing needs. In practical terms, this means some roles are being eliminated while others may be repurposed to focus on AI integration, product development or higher-value operational work.

For workers, the latest announcements highlight the uncertainty that often accompanies major technological transitions. The same tools that promise faster output and lower costs can also reshape job descriptions, reduce the need for certain functions and accelerate corporate restructuring. For employers, the challenge is to remain competitive without losing the specialized talent and institutional knowledge that helped build the industry in the first place.

The latest round of cuts also points to a wider recalibration in Israel’s tech landscape. After years in which scale and rapid expansion were often celebrated, companies now appear to be placing greater emphasis on durability, efficiency and adaptability. AI is driving much of that change, but it is not the only force at work. Macroeconomic pressures, currency movements and the need to maintain profitability are all feeding into the same decision-making process.

Taken together, the moves by Wix, Rapyd and Amdocs suggest that the current phase of the AI revolution is not simply about building new tools. It is also about reorganizing companies around a different set of assumptions about labor, automation and cost control. For Israeli technology firms, that transition is already producing visible consequences in the form of layoffs and restructuring, and more changes may follow if the pressures on the sector continue.
