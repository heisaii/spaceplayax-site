# Torc Robotics joins Mila in partnership on physical AI for trucks

Torc Robotics has formed a new partnership with Mila, the Quebec Artificial Intelligence Institute, in a move that links one of the best-known names in autonomous trucking with one of Canada’s leading AI research centers. The agreement makes Torc the only autonomous trucking company to join the institute and gives it dedicated research space in Montreal, according to the company announcement.

Torc is a subsidiary of Daimler Truck AG, and the partnership is framed around physical AI, a field focused on AI systems that operate in the real world rather than in purely digital environments. In transportation, that idea is especially relevant because automated vehicles must constantly interpret sensor data, understand dynamic road conditions, and make decisions that are safe, timely, and robust enough for everyday use.

By connecting with Mila, Torc is placing its autonomous trucking effort closer to an academic research environment known for deep expertise in machine learning. The arrangement suggests a collaboration intended to accelerate work at the intersection of research and product development, where advances in AI often need to be tested against the demands of physical systems, long-haul logistics, and road safety. The dedicated research space in Montreal also signals that the relationship is intended to be more than a symbolic affiliation.

The partnership comes as physical AI has become an increasingly important concept across robotics and transportation. Unlike software that only processes data on a screen, physical AI must interact with an environment that is unpredictable, noisy, and constantly changing. For autonomous trucks, that means handling everything from lane positioning and traffic flow to complex edge cases that can arise on highways and freight routes. Any progress in this area depends not only on larger datasets and better models, but also on close iteration between researchers and engineers working on real systems.

For Torc, the collaboration may help support its broader autonomous trucking development efforts within Daimler Truck’s portfolio. For Mila, the addition of an autonomous trucking company expands the institute’s industrial partnerships into a sector that has significant technical and operational complexity. The fact that Torc is the only autonomous trucking company represented at the institute gives the company a distinct position within that research network.

The announcement also reflects a broader trend in AI development: companies are increasingly looking for specialized research partnerships as the technology moves from abstract capability to deployed systems. In autonomous trucking, that shift is particularly important because the performance requirements are high and the operating environment is unforgiving. The combination of academic research, industrial engineering, and dedicated physical research space can help teams explore how models behave in realistic settings and how those systems might be improved over time.

While the announcement did not outline the full scope of the work to come, it is clear that the partnership is centered on advancing physical AI for autonomous trucks. The Montreal presence gives Torc a foothold inside a major AI research institution, while Mila gains an industry partner working on one of the most challenging applications for AI in motion. As autonomous trucking continues to evolve, collaborations like this are likely to play a significant role in shaping how quickly the technology can mature and how effectively it can be translated into commercial use.
